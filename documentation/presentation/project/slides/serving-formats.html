The serving benchmark of 17 November measured every serving path on the idle GPU for three models: the README classifier at 512 pixels, the detection benchmark run_9 at 1024 pixels and the sequence labelling tutorial run. The numbers are the classifier at batch 16 with one client over shared memory: 1,215 images per second with TensorRT FP16 through Triton, 646 with TensorRT FP32, 349 for the eager PyTorch model in the process, and TorchScript through Triton is not faster than eager (320). TensorRT fuses the convolution, batch normalization and ReLU of every block into one kernel and picks the fastest kernels for this GPU. evaluate_served_formats.py scored the test split through every served format: the FP32 paths within 0.01 points of the eager model, FP16 within 0.03 for the classifier and the sequence labeller. The detector lost 7.88 points of mAP@0.5 in FP16 because the group offsets of its exported NMS overflowed FP16; with one NonMaxSuppression batch per group and DIoU-NMS on boxes in fractions of the image, its FP16 and FP32 engines match the eager model (+0.21 and +0.01 points) at 189 images per second at batch 16. The exported dynamic batching suited none of the three models, and TensorRT engines cost about a minute of first requests for the detector. BF16 is refused by the Triton ONNX Runtime backend; INT8 and FP8 are not offered yet.
